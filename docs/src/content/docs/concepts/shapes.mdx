The way a particle is drawn depends on its _shape_. party.js ships with a few shapes that cover most effects, like squares, circles and stars, as well as text and images. You can also add your own shapes, as paths or custom draw functions. This page explains how shapes are sized and placed, which ones are built in and how to add your own.

## Shape space

All shapes are drawn in the same coordinate system, called **shape space**. A shape fits into a 1x1 box, centered at (0, 0), so it reaches from -0.5 to 0.5 in both directions. Like on a regular canvas, +y points down.

When a particle is drawn, this box is scaled to the particle's `size` in pixels, rotated by its orientation, and moved to its position. For example, a circle with a `size` of 10 will be drawn as 10 pixels wide, as would a square. Shapes that aren't as wide as they are tall, like a narrow strip, fill the box in their longer direction.

## Built-in shapes

The most common shapes for effects are built-in, and easily be used and configured.

### Primitives

| Shape | Looks like |
| --- | --- |
| [`square`](/api/particles/squareshape/) | A square, or a rectangle with an `aspectRatio`. `cornerRadius` rounds its corners. |
| [`circle`](/api/particles/circleshape/) | A circle. |
| [`star`](/api/particles/starshape/) | A star with 5 `points`, or as many as you like. |
| [`polygon`](/api/particles/polygonshape/) | A regular polygon with the given number of `sides`. |

`confetti()` uses rounded squares and circles by default, and `sparkles()` uses stars. Confetti's `shapes` option takes a list to pick from:

<LiveDemo demo="shapes-builtin" size="compact" label="Fire shapes" icon={ShapesIcon} />

Like other values, shapes are checked before they're used. A polygon with 2 sides or a negative corner radius throws a [`PartyJSError`](/api/utilities/partyjserror/), either when the effect is created, or for shapes that come from a sampler, when a particle spawns.

### Paths

For any shape you can describe as an outline, use a [`Path2D`](https://developer.mozilla.org/docs/Web/API/Path2D). The path has to be drawn in shape space, inside the 1 by 1 box around (0, 0):

<LiveDemo demo="shapes-path" size="compact" label="Fire plus signs" icon={PlusIcon} />

Icons are also a good source of shapes. Most icons are drawn on a larger grid, like 24 by 24 pixels, so they need to be scaled into the box first:

```js
// an icon from a 24x24 grid, moved to (0, 0) and scaled to a size of 1
const icon = new Path2D();
icon.addPath(new Path2D("M12 2 L22 22 L2 22 Z"), new DOMMatrix().scale(1 / 24).translate(-12, -12));
```

:::caution
Only create paths once, and reuse them for every particle. A sampler that creates a new path every time it's called builds the same shape over and over, which wastes time and memory.

Also, `Path2D` only exists in the browser. If your page is rendered on the server, creating a path at the top level of a module throws there. Create it in an event handler, or the first time an effect starts.
:::

### Text

Single characters or plain text can also be used as a shape.

You can also use emojis, however they will not be affected by the particle's `color` or lighting. Opacity works fine though.

<LiveDemo demo="shapes-emoji" size="compact" label="Fire emojis" icon={FaceSlightlySmilingIcon} />

:::caution
Emoji may wobble slightly while a particle rotates, depending on the browser, because browsers typically snap emoji to the pixel grid. At the sizes and speeds of typical effects it shouldn't be noticeable.
:::

### Images

Particles can also be pictures, like your logo. Give the image shape a URL, and party.js loads the image for you:

<LiveDemo demo="shapes-image" size="compact" label="Fire logos" icon={ImageIcon} />

An image keeps its aspect ratio, with its longer side filling the box. It also keeps its own colors: it fades out with the particle, but isn't tinted by its `color` or lit.

Until the image has loaded, its particles simply aren't drawn. Loading starts when the first particle spawns, so a large image can miss the first burst. To show it right away, preload it in your page's `<head>`:

```html
<link rel="preload" as="image" href="/logo.png" />
```

Instead of a URL, you can also pass anything a canvas can draw, like an `<img>` that has already loaded, a `<canvas>` or an `ImageBitmap`. If you pass an SVG as an `<img>`, give it a `width` and `height`, since some browsers report a size of 0 otherwise.

Each URL is only loaded once, and kept for later effects. If it can't be loaded, party.js reports it once in the console, and the effect plays without those particles.

## Custom drawing functions

A custom shape draws the particle with your own function, using the canvas' [context](https://developer.mozilla.org/docs/Web/API/CanvasRenderingContext2D). The context is already set up when your function is called: it's moved, rotated and scaled into shape space, and its `fillStyle` is the particle's lit color, including its opacity. Here, each particle is drawn as three circles, which makes it look like it glows:

<LiveDemo demo="shapes-glow" size="compact" label="Light up" icon={LightbulbIcon} />

`globalAlpha` is multiplied with the opacity of the `fillStyle`, so the glow still fades out with the particle. You don't need to reset anything you change, like `globalAlpha` here: the context is saved before your function is called, and restored afterwards.

Your function also receives the particle being drawn, so the drawing can change with it. Here, each particle is a ring that gets thinner as it ages:

<LiveDemo demo="shapes-ring" size="compact" label="Thinning rings" icon={CircleIcon} />

Only read from the particle. Drawing happens once per frame, not once per simulation step, so to change particles, write a [behavior](/concepts/behaviors/#custom-behaviors).

Keep custom drawing functions quick, they run for every particle, on every frame. Prepare anything you can, like images or gradients, outside of the function.

## From 3D to 2D

Each particle has a position, a size, and an orientation in 3D. When it's drawn, party.js takes its shape from [shape space](#shape-space), scales it to the particle's size, turns it by its orientation, and then looks at the result straight from the front:

- **Its position** becomes a point on the canvas, measured from the effect's origin. Particle space has +y pointing up, the canvas has it pointing down, so party.js flips it.
- **Its depth is ignored.** There's no perspective, so a particle doesn't get smaller as it moves away, and particles don't hide each other based on their depth. Particles are drawn in the order they spawned, so newer ones end up on top.
- **Its orientation** decides how the flat shape lies in space. Facing you, it's drawn as it is. As it turns away, it gets narrower, until it's only a line when you see it edge-on, and then it shows its back side.
